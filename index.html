Visas

  • Subclass 417 — Working Holiday visa
  • Subclass 462 — Work and Holiday visa

To apply for a second or third visa, holders must usually complete a prescribed minimum period of specified work in certain industries and areas. Home Affairs describes this as at least 3 months (88 calendar days) of specified work on a first visa for a second visa, and at least 6 months (179 calendar days) of specified work on a second visa for a third visa. The two visas do not always use the same area rules. UK passport holders who lodge a 417 application on or after 1 July 2024 do not need specified work for a second or third 417 visa.

FAQ

What is specified work?
Specified work is work undertaken in a specified industry and area of Australia. The kinds of work and the areas are set by Home Affairs and by legal instruments LIN 22/012 (417) and LIN 22/013 (462). Always confirm the current rules on the official Home Affairs pages.
Why are 417 and 462 results different?
The two visas do not have the same specified-work rules. For subclass 462, mining is not specified work anywhere in Australia, and fishing and pearling and tree farming and felling count only in Northern Australia.
Does my home address postcode matter?
No. What matters is where the work is actually done. Use the postcode of the farm, site or business where you work.
I have a UK passport. Do I need specified work?
For the subclass 417 visa, UK passport holders who lodge an application on or after 1 July 2024 do not need to complete specified work for a second or third Working Holiday visa. See the Home Affairs arrangements for UK passport holders.
